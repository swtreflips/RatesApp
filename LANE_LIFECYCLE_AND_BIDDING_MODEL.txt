  Design document — March 31, 2026

________________________________________________________________________________
1. LANE LIFECYCLE — THE TTL MODEL
________________________________________________________________________________

  A lane is a unique POL + FD pair (e.g., Nhava Sheva → Los Angeles).

  When a requester posts a lane, it gets a TIME TO LIVE (TTL):

    - Default TTL: 14 days from the post date
    - The lane is "active" for that entire window
    - Providers can see and submit rates for any active lane
    - After TTL expires, the lane disappears from the provider view
    - All rate history stays permanently in the database

  KEY RULE: There is only ever ONE active lane per POL + FD.
  No duplicates. The lane is the persistent entity.


  Lifecycle states (driven by time, not manual status):

    ACTIVE      expires_at > now()     Providers see it. Rates can be submitted.
    EXPIRED     expires_at <= now()    Providers don't see it. History preserved.

  No manual "close" needed. Time handles it.


________________________________________________________________________________
2. DUPLICATE HANDLING — THE EXTENSION MODEL
________________________________________________________________________________

  What happens when a requester posts a lane that already exists and is active?

  SCENARIO: Nhava Sheva → LA was posted on Feb 1st (expires Feb 15th).
  On Feb 9th, the requester posts the same lane again.

  BEHAVIOR:
    1. System detects: "This lane is already active (expires in 6 days)"
    2. Warning shown: "Extend this lane for another 14 days?"
    3. If accepted:
       - expires_at is updated to Feb 23rd (Feb 9th + 14 days)
       - The lane stays as one record — no duplicate created
       - Previous rates remain in the DB as historical records
       - On the provider side: the lane appears with empty rate fields
         signaling "fresh rates needed for the next period"

  WHAT THE PROVIDER SEES:
    - The lane never disappeared — it just got extended
    - But the rate fields reset to empty after the extension
    - This is the signal: "the requester needs updated pricing"
    - The provider can see their previous rates in history,
      but the active view shows: "no current rate submitted"

  IF THE LANE HAS ALREADY EXPIRED:
    - Requester posts the same POL + FD
    - No warning — it's a fresh lane with a new TTL
    - Previous rates stay attached to the old period
    - This is effectively a new request cycle


________________________________________________________________________________
3. THE REQUESTER GRID — DEFAULT DASHBOARD VIEW
________________________________________________________________________________

  The requester's default view answers: "What's going on with my lanes?"

  GRID COLUMNS:

    #  | POL           | Final Dest    | Posted   | Expires  | Days Left | Rates | Status
    ---|---------------|---------------|----------|----------|-----------|-------|--------
    1  | Nhava Sheva   | Los Angeles   | Feb 1    | Feb 15   | 6         | 3     | Active
    2  | Mundra        | Dallas, TX    | Feb 3    | Feb 17   | 8         | 0     | Pending
    3  | Shanghai      | Chicago, IL   | Feb 5    | Feb 19   | 10        | 1     | Active
    4  | Nhava Sheva   | Seymour, IN   | Jan 20   | Feb 3    | -         | 2     | Expired

  COLUMN DEFINITIONS:

    Posted      — date the lane was first created (or last extended)
    Expires     — the TTL expiration date
    Days Left   — countdown (expires_at - today). Highlights urgency.
    Rates       — count of rate submissions received for this lane
    Status      — derived from time + rate count:

      "Pending"   = active lane, 0 rates received
      "Active"    = active lane, 1+ rates received
      "Expired"   = TTL has passed

  FILTERING / TABS:

    Default view:   Active lanes only (expires_at > now())
    Secondary tab:  All lanes (including expired, for history)

  SORTING:
    Default sort by Days Left ascending — lanes expiring soonest at top.
    This naturally surfaces urgency.


  HOW THE GRID CLEARS AFTER TTL:

    Day 1  (Feb 1):   Lane posted. Grid shows: Pending, 0 rates, 14 days left.
    Day 3  (Feb 3):   Provider submits rate. Grid shows: Active, 1 rate, 12 days.
    Day 14 (Feb 15):  TTL expires. Lane moves from "Active" tab to "All" tab.
                       Status shows "Expired". Rates still visible in history.

    The lane doesn't get deleted — it just stops appearing in the default
    active view. Providers no longer see it. Rate data is permanent.


  EXTENDING A LANE (from the grid):

    The requester can click an "Extend" or "Request Update" action on any
    active lane. This:
      1. Adds 14 days to expires_at
      2. Signals providers that fresh rates are needed
      3. Previous rates stay in history but the "current period" is empty

    From the grid perspective:
      Before extend:  Nhava Sheva → LA | Feb 1  | Feb 15 | 6 days | 3 rates | Active
      After extend:   Nhava Sheva → LA | Feb 9  | Feb 23 | 14 days | 0 rates | Pending

    The "Posted" date updates to the extension date.
    Rate count resets to 0 for the current period (old rates are historical).
    Status goes back to "Pending" because no rates exist for the new period.


________________________________________________________________________________
4. THE PROVIDER GRID — WHAT FORWARDERS SEE
________________________________________________________________________________

  Providers see all active lanes (expires_at > now()):

    #  | POL           | Final Dest    | Requested  | Days Left | My Rate
    ---|---------------|---------------|------------|-----------|--------
    1  | Nhava Sheva   | Los Angeles   | Feb 1      | 6         | $2,000
    2  | Mundra        | Dallas, TX    | Feb 3      | 8         | —
    3  | Shanghai      | Chicago, IL   | Feb 5      | 10        | —

  "My Rate" shows the provider's most recent rate for the current period.
  A dash means they haven't submitted yet.

  WHEN A LANE IS EXTENDED:
    - The lane stays in their grid (it didn't expire)
    - "My Rate" clears to "—" (previous rate is historical, new period)
    - "Days Left" resets based on new expires_at
    - The provider knows: "this lane needs fresh rates from me"

  EXPIRED LANES:
    Simply disappear from the provider grid. No action needed.

5. THE BIDDING SYSTEM — COMPETITIVE RATE UPDATES
________________________________________________________________________________

  THE CONCEPT:
    A provider can update their rate on an active lane at any time
    before the TTL expires. This creates a competitive bidding dynamic.

  HOW IT WORKS:

    Feb 1:  Requester posts Nhava Sheva → LA (TTL: 14 days)
    Feb 2:  Forwarder A submits: $2,000 (valid Feb 1–14)
    Feb 3:  Forwarder B submits: $1,950 (valid Feb 1–14)
    Feb 5:  Forwarder A sees they're being undercut.
            Forwarder A submits: $1,900 (valid Feb 1–14)

  WHAT HAPPENS IN THE DATABASE:
    - Forwarder A now has TWO rate records for this lane:
        Rate 1: $2,000, submitted Feb 2  (historical)
        Rate 2: $1,900, submitted Feb 5  (current / latest)
    - Both records are preserved — nothing is overwritten
    - The system treats the LATEST submission as the active rate

  WHAT THE REQUESTER SEES:
    In the "Received Rates" or lane detail view:

      Forwarder A:
        $1,900  (current — submitted Feb 5)
        $2,000  (previous — submitted Feb 2)  ← shown as strikethrough or dimmed

      Forwarder B:
        $1,950  (current — submitted Feb 3)

    The requester can see the bid history: Forwarder A started at $2,000
    and dropped to $1,900. This is valuable competitive intelligence.

  WHAT THE PROVIDER SEES:
    In their own view, they see their latest rate. They can submit a
    new rate at any time to update their bid. Their previous rates
    are visible to them as history.

  RULES:
    - A provider can only bid on ACTIVE lanes (expires_at > now())
    - A new rate submission doesn't replace the old one — it creates
      a new record. The latest record is the "current" bid.
    - Rate validity (valid_from, valid_until) is attached to each
      rate record independently. The provider sets these dates.
    - There is no minimum or maximum number of bid updates.
    - The lane TTL controls the bidding window. When the lane expires,
      no more bids can be submitted.

  WHY THIS SCALES:
    - It's just INSERT, never UPDATE. Append-only rate history.
    - "Current rate" = most recent rate record per provider per lane.
      Simple query: ORDER BY created_at DESC LIMIT 1 per provider.
    - Discounts are naturally visible: compare a provider's first
      and latest rate for the same lane.
    - No special "bidding mode" or auction logic needed.
      The TTL window IS the auction. Rates ARE the bids.
    - Works identically whether 1 provider or 20 providers are quoting.


________________________________________________________________________________
6. HOW BIDDING INTERACTS WITH LANE EXTENSION
________________________________________________________________________________

  When a lane is extended (TTL pushed out), the bidding resets:

    BEFORE EXTENSION:
      Forwarder A: $1,900 (submitted Feb 5)
      Forwarder B: $1,950 (submitted Feb 3)

    LANE EXTENDED ON FEB 9 (new expiry: Feb 23):

    AFTER EXTENSION:
      Forwarder A: — (no current rate for new period)
      Forwarder B: — (no current rate for new period)

    Previous rates ($1,900, $1,950) are preserved as history
    from the first period. But the new period starts clean.
    Providers must submit fresh rates.

  WHY THIS MAKES SENSE:
    - Market rates change between periods
    - The extension signals: "I need current pricing"
    - Providers may have different rates for the next half-month
    - Starting clean prevents stale pricing from carrying forward
    - The requester can still VIEW previous period rates for reference

7. DATA MODEL IMPLICATIONS
________________________________________________________________________________

  LANE TABLE (rate_request_lanes):
    id              uuid PK
    batch_id        uuid FK → rate_request_batches (how it was born)
    pol             text
    fd              text
    posted_at       timestamptz        (when created or last extended)
    expires_at      timestamptz        (posted_at + 14 days)
    period          integer default 1  (increments on each extension)
    created_at      timestamptz        (original creation, never changes)

  RATES TABLE:
    id              uuid PK
    lane_id         uuid FK → rate_request_lanes
    provider_id     uuid FK → auth.users
    period          integer            (matches lane.period at submission time)
    pol             text
    pod             text
    last_cy         text
    fd              text
    carrier         text
    rate_amount     numeric
    currency        text
    transit_days    integer
    valid_from      date
    valid_until     date
    created_at      timestamptz

  KEY QUERIES:

    Active lanes (provider view):
      SELECT * FROM rate_request_lanes WHERE expires_at > now()

    Current rates for a lane (latest per provider, current period):
      SELECT DISTINCT ON (provider_id)
        * FROM rates
        WHERE lane_id = X AND period = (lane's current period)
        ORDER BY provider_id, created_at DESC

    Bid history for a lane:
      SELECT * FROM rates
        WHERE lane_id = X
        ORDER BY created_at DESC

    Pending lanes (requester default view):
      SELECT l.*, COUNT(r.id) as rate_count
        FROM rate_request_lanes l
        LEFT JOIN rates r ON r.lane_id = l.id AND r.period = l.period
        WHERE l.expires_at > now()
        GROUP BY l.id
        ORDER BY l.expires_at ASC


________________________________________________________________________________
8. SUMMARY — THE THREE PILLARS
________________________________________________________________________________

  1. TTL MODEL
     Lanes live for 14 days. Time controls visibility.
     No manual close needed. Extensions add 14 more days.
     One active lane per POL + FD. No duplicates.

  2. PERIOD SYSTEM
     Each TTL cycle is a "period." Extension = new period.
     Rates are tagged with the period they belong to.
     Current period rates are "active." Previous periods are history.

  3. COMPETITIVE BIDDING
     Providers can update their rate anytime during the TTL window.
     New rates are appended, not overwritten. Latest = current bid.
     Lane extension resets all bids — fresh pricing for new period.
     The TTL window IS the bidding window. No separate auction logic.
